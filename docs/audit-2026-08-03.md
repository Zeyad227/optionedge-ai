## 1. Executive summary

The application is better engineered than most projects of its kind. Data provenance is enforced at the type level (`Sourced[T]` cannot be constructed without naming a source), explanations are validated against the factor list that produced them, disclaimers are consistent, and the README is candid about rate limits and free-tier data quality.

The trading logic, however, was fundamentally broken in a way that produced actively harmful recommendations. The scoring engine conflated three different kinds of evidence, and as a result **it recommended buying a stock in freefall**. This was not a hypothetical edge case; it was reproduced deterministically and is now covered by regression tests.

The most consequential structural finding is that **nothing in the system has ever been validated against history**. `Backtester` and `LabelGenerator` exist only as abstract classes with `TODO` bodies. Every `confidence` number the app has ever emitted was an arbitrary weighted blend with no relationship to realised outcomes.

## 2. The headline bug: Buy on an 81% decline

A synthetic series declining 81.3% over 140 bars — an unambiguous collapse — returned `action=Buy`, `trend=Bullish`, and generated a long entry with a stop-loss and two upside targets.

### Reproduction (pre-fix)

```
Price path: 296.40 -> 55.35 (-81.3% over 140 bars)

  ema_9        = 58.173
  ema_20       = 62.566
  ema_50       = 78.311
  rsi          = 0.0
  macd         = -5.944
  macd_signal  = -6.247
  adx          = 100.0

FACTOR                        DIRECTION    WEIGHT   CONTRIB
-----------------------------------------------------------
EMA stack                     bearish        1.20     -1.20
RSI                           bullish        1.00     +1.00
MACD                          bullish        1.00     +1.00
ADX                           neutral        0.60     +0.20
-----------------------------------------------------------
SUM                                          3.80     +1.00

normalised score = +0.2632   (Buy if >= +0.25)

>>> ACTION = Buy   TREND = Bullish   CONFIDENCE = 33.4%
>>> entry=55.3469 stop_loss=53.3609 take_profit=[57.9949, 59.3189]
```

Three independent defects stacked to clear the +0.25 threshold.

### 2.1 RSI mean-reversion applied without regard to regime

`rsi < 30` unconditionally contributed **+1.00 bullish**. In a sustained downtrend RSI pins near zero for months. The reading means sellers are in complete control — it is continuation evidence, not a reversal signal. Applying range-bound mean-reversion logic inside a trend inverts the indicator's meaning.

### 2.2 MACD crossover evaluated without zero-line context

The check was `macd > macd_signal and hist > 0` → **+1.00 bullish**. Here MACD was −5.944 against a signal of −6.247. Both lines sit far below zero; the line merely crossed above its own signal. That is decelerating downside momentum, not a bull signal. The code never asked which side of zero the crossover occurred on.

### 2.3 ADX contributed signed direction while being unsigned

```python
if adx >= 25:
    _factor("ADX", f"{adx:.1f} trending", "neutral", 0.6, 0.2, ...)
else:
    _factor("ADX", f"{adx:.1f} weak trend", "neutral", 0.6, -0.2, ...)
```

ADX measures how strongly a market trends, not which way. This added a fixed **+0.20 bullish tilt to every strongly trending symbol**, including collapsing ones, and a bearish tilt to every choppy one. Confirmed empirically — identical `+0.20` on both a strong uptrend and a strong downtrend:

```
uptrend    ADX observed=100.0 trending         contribution=+0.20
downtrend  ADX observed=100.0 trending         contribution=+0.20
```

### 2.4 The same category error in two more inputs

**Relative volume** contributed `+0.60 bullish` for any bar with volume ≥ 1.5× average, regardless of whether price rose or fell. A high-volume capitulation selloff was scored as buying pressure:

```
--- 3x volume capitulation on a sustained downtrend ---
    Relative volume    observed=2.73x average    direction=bullish  contribution=+0.60
    raw score sum = +1.600   action=Buy  confidence=41.0
```

**IV rank** contributed `+0.50 bullish` when below 30 and `−0.30 bearish` when above 70. Volatility level says nothing about direction; it informs whether to buy or sell premium. Confirmed that IV rank alone moved the directional score by 0.8 on an identical bearish series:

```
  iv_rank=   10 -> contribution=+0.50 | raw=+1.500 action=Buy   conf=42.1
  iv_rank=   50 -> contribution=+0.00 | raw=+1.000 action=Avoid conf=35.4
  iv_rank=   90 -> contribution=-0.30 | raw=+0.700 action=Avoid conf=29.6
```

### 2.5 Neutral factors diluted the score

`max_abs = sum(abs(f.weight) for f in factors)` included factors whose contribution was always exactly zero — support/resistance levels (weight 0.3 each) and doji candles (weight 0.5). Measured effect of a single doji:

```
raw contribution sum        = 1.4000
denominator (all weights)   = 4.3000
  dead weight within it     = 0.5000  (11.6% of the denominator)

score as implemented        = +0.3256
score w/o dead weight       = +0.3684
```

A doji is indecision by definition and cannot cast a directional vote, yet it reduced signal magnitude by 11.6% — enough to flip borderline calls to `Avoid`.

### The fix

Evidence is now separated into three roles, declared on the `Factor` model:

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `directional` | Casts a signed vote; counts toward the score denominator |
| `modulator` | Unsigned; scales conviction only (ADX, IV rank) |
| `context` | Reported for the reader; never affects arithmetic (S/R levels, doji) |

Plus regime-aware readings:

- A `trend_regime` is derived from EMA structure combined with ±DI and ADX (`uptrend` / `downtrend` / `range` / `unknown`).
- RSI flips meaning by regime: oversold in a downtrend is **bearish** continuation; overbought in an uptrend is **bullish** momentum; mean-reversion applies only in a range.
- MACD crossovers below the zero line earn 40% weight and are described as "downside momentum easing" rather than "bullish crossover".
- Relative volume follows the direction of the bar that produced it.
- ADX and IV rank become modulators with zero weight and zero contribution. ADX scales confidence (×1.15 when trending, ×0.8 when choppy). IV rank surfaces as a `volatility_regime` field (`cheap` / `mid` / `expensive`).
- A **coverage gate** (`MIN_COVERAGE_FOR_DIRECTION = 0.45`) refuses a directional call when too many input families are missing, rather than extrapolating from a handful of indicators.

### Result (post-fix)

```
FACTOR                        DIRECTION    WEIGHT   CONTRIB
-----------------------------------------------------------
EMA stack                     bearish        1.20     -1.20
RSI                           bearish        1.00     -0.40
MACD                          bullish        1.00     +0.40
ADX                           neutral        0.00     +0.00
Relative volume               neutral        0.00     +0.00
-----------------------------------------------------------
SUM                                          3.20     -1.20

normalised score = -0.3750
missing factors  = ['news', 'insider_cluster', 'option_chain', 'iv_rank',
                    'insufficient_factor_coverage']

>>> ACTION = Avoid   TREND = Bearish   CONFIDENCE = 40.4%
>>> entry=55.3469 stop_loss=None take_profit=[]
```

The score moved from **+0.263 to −0.375**. The action is `Avoid` rather than `Sell` because coverage is 3/7 ≈ 43%, below the gate — with four of eight input families unavailable, declining to take a side is the correct behaviour. MACD retains a reduced `+0.40`: downside momentum genuinely is easing, and that is now stated proportionately.

## 3. Insider clustering was reading payroll as conviction

```python
_BUY_CODES = {"P", "A", "M"}   # purchase, grant, exercise
_SELL_CODES = {"S", "D", "F"}  # sale, disposition, tax
```

Of the SEC Form 4 transaction codes, only two reflect a decision an insider chose to make with their own money:

| Code | Meaning | Signal? |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Open-market or private purchase | **Yes — bullish** |
| **S** | Open-market or private sale | **Yes — bearish** |
| A | Grant, award or other acquisition from issuer | No — compensation |
| M | Exercise or conversion of a derivative | No — mechanical, often pre-planned |
| F | Shares withheld to pay exercise price or tax | No — mechanical, forced |
| D | Disposition back to the issuer | No — buyback/forfeiture, not a market sale |
| G | Bona fide gift | No — estate planning |
| C, X | Conversion / derivative exercise | No |

### Verified consequences

```
4 executives receiving RSU grants (code A) -> clusters:
  [{'symbol': 'ACME', 'direction': 'Bullish', 'insider_count': 4,
    'label': 'Cluster buy: 4 insiders / 4 filings'}]

4 execs with shares withheld for tax (code F) ->
  [{'symbol': 'ACME', 'direction': 'Bearish', 'insider_count': 4,
    'label': 'Cluster sell: 4 insiders / 4 filings'}]
```

A routine annual RSU grant to four executives produced a bullish cluster contributing **+0.9**. Those same executives having shares withheld to pay the tax on that grant produced a bearish cluster contributing **−0.9**. Both are calendar artifacts of the same compensation event.

Because grants and exercises vastly outnumber open-market purchases in the Form 4 population, the large majority of detected "clusters" were vesting-cycle noise. This is the classic insider-data error; the academic literature (Lakonishok & Lee; Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski) is consistent that predictive content is confined to open-market purchases, with a further distinction between "routine" and "opportunistic" filers.

The mapping was **duplicated** in `providers/sec_edgar.py` (`{"P","A"}` → Bullish, `{"S","D"}` → Bearish), so a fix in one place would have been silently overridden by the other. Both now call a single `code_sentiment()` helper.

One subtlety handled: `_trade_sentiment` previously honoured any pre-set `trade.sentiment` before consulting the code, so stale labels written by the old provider mapping would survive. A recognised code is now authoritative; a pre-set sentiment only fills the gap when the code is absent or unrecognised (as with the Atom feed's `"Form 4 filing"` placeholder).

## 4. Other verified defects, fixed

### 4.1 Put/call ratio and max pain blended every expiration

Open interest accumulates over a contract's entire life, so a single LEAP strike can carry orders of magnitude more OI than an entire weekly expiry. Test chain: front expiry call-heavy (10,000 call OI vs 100 put OI), plus one distant LEAP put with 500,000 OI.

```
put_call_ratio (all expiries blended) = 50.01
put_call_ratio (near expiry only)     = 0.01   <- the honest number
max_pain       (all expiries blended) = 200.0
max_pain       (near expiry only)     = 100.0
```

The blended reading was **inverted** — the front month was overwhelmingly call-heavy, but the metric reported extreme put-heaviness. Max pain across expirations is meaningless in principle, since options settle on their own dates and payoffs cannot be summed across them.

Both metrics now scope to the front expiration by default, with `all_expirations=True` available and `chain_summary` reporting `front_expiration` and `front_contract_count` so the reader knows which slice produced the number. The put/call factor was also relabelled as open-interest *positioning* rather than *flow* (true flow requires intraday volume) and its weight reduced from 0.8 to 0.5.

### 4.2 RSI returned None on an unbroken up-run

```python
rs = avg_gain / avg_loss.replace(0, np.nan)
```

An unbroken run of up-bars drives `avg_loss` to zero. Masking that to NaN discarded the strongest possible reading precisely when it was most informative — the factor was dropped and `"rsi"` was appended to `missing_factors`.

```
RSI on 40 consecutive up bars -> nan   (expected 100.0)
```

The zero-division is now left to produce `inf` (RSI 100), with a genuinely flat series resolving to 50.

### 4.3 VWAP was a cumulative average over the entire series

```python
return (typical * df["volume"]).cumsum() / df["volume"].cumsum()
```

On a 200-bar series that steps from 100 to 300 for the final 20 bars:

```
Last close = 300.0
VWAP       = 120.00   <- dragged down by 180 stale bars
```

On a daily chart, "VWAP" means a trailing window; a whole-history cumulative average converges on a long-run mean and is not what a trader reads. `compute_all` now reports the trailing 20-session figure as `vwap` and preserves the original as `vwap_anchored`. The `vwap()` function itself is unchanged (still valid as an anchored VWAP when the series start is meaningful) so the existing golden test remains honest.

### 4.4 Alpaca `volume` was populated from `open_interest`

```python
volume=_i(meta.get("open_interest")),
open_interest=_i(meta.get("open_interest")),
```

Contracts traded today conflated with total contracts outstanding — two different metrics, one of them silently wrong. Now read from the snapshot's `dailyBar.v`.

### 4.5 The "partial lookback" flag could never be false

```python
"partial": rows[0].as_of > cutoff or rows[0].as_of > ALPACA_IV_EARLIEST
```

`ALPACA_IV_EARLIEST` is 2024-02-01, so the second clause is true for any date after that. Every IV rank reading was labelled "(partial lookback)" and the warning became noise the user learns to ignore.

```
first snapshot     = 2025-08-03  (a complete year of history)
partial flag       = True   <- should be False
  clause 1 (first > cutoff)             = False
  clause 2 (first > ALPACA_IV_EARLIEST) = True  <- always True after Feb 2024
```

History cannot begin before Alpaca's data floor, so that date is a floor, not a defect. Now `rows[0].as_of > max(cutoff, ALPACA_IV_EARLIEST)`.

## 6. Why "accuracy" is undefined, not merely low

`app/stubs.py` contains:

```python
class Backtester(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    async def run(self, config: BacktestConfig) -> BacktestResult:
        """TODO: simulate strategies; report robustness metrics, never claim guarantees."""


class LabelGenerator(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    async def generate(self, config: BacktestConfig) -> list[dict[str, Any]]:
        """TODO: replay factors forward over Alpaca option bars (from Feb 2024)."""
```

Both are abstract with `TODO` bodies and no implementations anywhere in the codebase. Consequently:

- No signal has ever been compared against a realised outcome.
- The factor weights (1.2 for EMA stack, 1.0 for RSI, 0.9 for insider cluster, …) were chosen by hand and have never been fitted or validated.
- `confidence` was an arbitrary blend: `abs(score) * 70 + coverage * 30`.

Assigning an accuracy score would fabricate a measurement. The honest statement is that accuracy is **unmeasured**, and that this is the single most important gap relative to the stated goal of improving prediction accuracy — every other predictive improvement is unfalsifiable until a backtest exists.

Interim mitigations added so the number cannot be misread:

- `confidence_basis: Literal["heuristic_uncalibrated", "backtest_calibrated"]` on `SignalResponse`, defaulting to `heuristic_uncalibrated`.
- `confidence_breakdown: list[ConfidenceComponent]` decomposing the number into additive, attributable percentage points per factor plus a data-coverage component.
- Confidence ceiling lowered from 95 to 90, so it never reads like a near-certainty.

## 7. Pushback: the trade card is internally inconsistent

The requested trade card specifies, for a single position:

```
Entry Price:            $0.10
Take Profit:            $3.00
Expected Return:        +2900%
Risk/Reward:            1 : 30
Probability of Profit:  87%
Confidence:             94%
AI Rating:              ★★★★★
```

**1:30 risk/reward and 87% probability of profit cannot both be true.**

For any bet with win probability `p` and payoff ratio `b:1`, expected value is `p·b − (1−p)`. Setting EV to zero gives the breakeven win probability:

```
p = 1 / (1 + b)
```

At `b = 30`, that is `1/31 ≈ 3.2%`. A fairly priced 30:1 payoff carries roughly a **3% probability of profit**. Asserting 87% claims an edge of about 27× over a liquid, market-maker-quoted options market. A $0.10 option reaching $3.00 is a lottery ticket, and the honest probability of profit for one is single digits.

Probability of profit *can* be computed legitimately — Black-Scholes is already implemented in `analysis/greeks.py`, and risk-neutral probability of finishing in the money is approximated by delta, which the chain already carries. That computation will print roughly 3–8% for contracts like the one above. It is the correct number and it contradicts the mock, which is why it needs a product decision before the card is built around it.

Two other elements of the specification cannot be honestly produced today:

- **"AI Rating: ★★★★★"** — a five-star scale implies a ranking validated against outcomes.
- **"Historically, this setup has resulted in upward movement 82% of the time over the next two trading sessions"** — this sentence requires exactly the backtest that does not exist. Generating it from nothing would be fabrication presented as evidence.

Finally, on the stated target: **no system can reliably deliver 80%+ directional accuracy in live markets.** Any product surface implying it is a liability rather than a feature. Note that high *win rate* and high *expected value* are largely independent — a 90%-win-rate strategy that loses 30× on the tenth trade is a losing strategy, and headline win rate is the metric most prone to inviting that structure.

## 8. Verified but not yet fixed, prioritized

### P0 — Reliability and performance

| Issue | Location | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| New `httpx.AsyncClient` per request | `providers/http.py:73` | No connection pooling; a fresh TLS handshake on every provider call |
| Sequential universe scan | `services/market.py:313` | 12 symbols × multiple calls each, serially, with a 30s timeout and no circuit breaker — one dead provider can stall a scan for minutes |
| FinBERT runs inside the event loop | `ai/sentiment.py:80` | Synchronous transformer inference blocks **every** concurrent request for the duration |
| No negative caching | `cache/redis_cache.py:156` | `None` results are never cached, so a failing provider is retried on every single request |
| N+1 queries | `db/repository.py:27,62` | One `SELECT` per article and per filing inside the write loop |
| `asyncio.create_task` reference dropped | `main.py:29` | Task can be garbage-collected mid-flight |
| Duplicate cluster computation | `services/market.py:191` | `detect_clusters` runs twice per `build_signal`, including on cache hits |
| Sequential `ticker_detail` | `services/market.py:332` | Five awaits that could be gathered |
| Unbounded `scan_results` growth | `db/repository.py:90` | ~3,456 rows/day with full JSON payloads, no retention policy |
| Sentiment cache absent | `ai/sentiment.py` | Docstring claims "cached by URL hash"; `lru_cache` is imported but unused and `url_hash` is never called, so every refresh re-runs inference on the same articles |

### P1 — Correctness

- **`date.today()` used for market dates in 7 places** (`greeks.py:111`, `insider_clusters.py:71`, `iv_history.py:151,287,331,487`) — ruff flags each as `DTZ011`. Running after 19:00 ET rolls the session date forward a day.
- **Workers run in every uvicorn process.** `--workers 4` starts four schedulers and quadruples third-party quota consumption. Needs leader election or a separate worker process.
- **No market-hours awareness.** The scanner refreshes every 5 minutes, 24/7, including weekends and holidays, burning quota on stale data.
- **`health` reports configuration, not health.** `registry.status()` returns static booleans (`"yfinance": True` unconditionally), which the dashboard renders as live provider status.
- **IV rank mixes incompatible sources.** Backfilled values invert Black-Scholes on ~1-week-DTE ATM calls from Alpaca bars; live values take ATM IV from a chain of arbitrary DTE via a different provider. Ranking a mixture of term structures compares unlike quantities.
- **yfinance uses `auto_adjust=True`.** Adjusted closes are then used as spot when inverting Black-Scholes against unadjusted strikes, biasing backfilled IV for dividend payers.
- **Alpaca chain pagination ignored.** `limit: 1000` with no `next_page_token` handling silently truncates chains for high-strike-count underlyings such as SPY.
- **Nearest expiry chosen by default.** `dates[0]` can be 0DTE or 1DTE, whose IV and greeks are the least suitable for a multi-hour signal.
- **Candlestick patterns lack trend context.** A hammer is only bullish after a decline; the detector fires regardless of what preceded it.

### P2 — Security

- **No authentication anywhere.** Every endpoint is open.
- **Unauthenticated `/api/scanner` triggers a full universe scan**, making third-party quota exhaustion trivial for anyone who can reach the port.
- **No rate limiting** of any kind.
- **No input validation on `symbol` path parameters** — arbitrary strings flow into outbound provider URLs.
- **No security headers, no request-ID logging, no global exception handler.**
- `assert` used for control flow in `db/session.py:44,51` — stripped under `python -O`.

Handled correctly: `.env` is gitignored (verified via `git check-ignore`), no secrets are committed, SQLAlchemy ORM is used throughout with no raw SQL, and CORS origins are configurable rather than wildcarded.

### P3 — Frontend

- **`NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL` defaults to `http://localhost:8000`** (`lib/api.ts:3`). All data fetching happens in server components, so in production this resolves to the deployment host and every page fails.
- **`s.confidence.toFixed(0)` crashes the entire scanner route** (`scanner/page.tsx:149`) if the API returns null — unlike other pages, which degrade gracefully.
- **Silent failure on scanner, news, and insiders pages.** A backend outage is indistinguishable from genuinely empty data.
- **Dashboard error banner misreports partial failure** — shows "Backend unreachable" whenever `scanner` is null, even if news and insiders loaded fine.
- **Accessibility gaps:** no `<caption>`/`scope` on data tables, no focus-visible rings, no `aria-current` on nav, no `aria-label` on provider status badges (state conveyed by colour plus `line-through` alone), no text alternative for the chart canvas.
- **Option chain silently truncates** from 200 fetched to 40 displayed with no indication.
- **No mobile navigation pattern** — four horizontal links in a sticky header.
- **New `confidence_breakdown` and `confidence_basis` fields are not rendered.** The UI still shows only the bare percentage.

### P4 — Tooling

- **CI lint is currently failing.** `ruff>=0.8.0` floated to 0.16.1, which enables additional rule groups; 34 pre-existing violations now surface. Pin the version.
- `iv_history.py` has every line double-spaced from a write artifact — ~250 lines of logic spread across 528 lines. Left untouched per the no-whitespace-changes rule.
- Unused dependency `lucide-react` in `frontend/package.json`.
- `force-dynamic` combined with `next: { revalidate: 30 }` sends mixed signals about freshness.

## 10. Methodology

Findings were not asserted from reading alone. Each claim in §2–§4 was tested against the real code via throwaway harnesses that printed factor-level decompositions, and each was either confirmed or discarded.

One hypothesis was **discarded on evidence**: the initial claim that support/resistance factors dilute the score could not be reproduced with a smooth synthetic uptrend, because that series generated no pivots and therefore no S/R factors. Re-testing with a choppy series and a forced doji confirmed the mechanism at 11.6% dead weight, which is the figure reported.

The harnesses (`verify_audit.py`, `verify_dilution.py`, `verify_direction.py`, `verify_downtrend.py`) were deleted after their findings were converted into permanent regression tests.
